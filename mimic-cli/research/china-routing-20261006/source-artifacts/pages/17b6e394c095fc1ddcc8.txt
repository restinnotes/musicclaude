AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon's smartphone promotions push Q2 sales performance – report
Carrier promotions have played an important role in increasing smartphone sell-through rates for the Big Three mobile operators in Q2 of 2025, according to Counterpoint Research.
Kelsey Ziser,Senior Editor,Light Reading
August 19, 2025
4 Min Read
(Source: Stephen Barnes/Technology/Alamy Stock Photo)
Wireless carriers' promotions have boosted Q2 sales in the US smartphone market, according to a new report by Counterpoint Research.
Wireless carriers are a "very large channel" for selling smartphones to end users, Jason Leigh, senior research manager for IDC, told Light Reading earlier this year.
"The phone bundles are the biggest carrot that the carriers can offer, and they've been willing over the last five years or so to really lean into that to either retain customers or gain new ones," Leigh said.
Smartphone sell-through for AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon stores increased by 16%, 20% and 12%, respectively, year-over-year (YoY) in Q2, boosted by summer device promotions, according to Counterpoint Research.
Upgrade rates were also strong for the Big Three carriers, and sell-through in the premium device segment increased 11% YoY. The average selling price (ASP) of a US smartphone in Q2 increased by about $15 to $647.53, up from $632.59 last year.
Related:Nothing is onto something
However, demand for handsets in the mid-range price segment of $300-$599, such as the iPhone 16e and Google Pixel 9a, has increased 48%.
Overall, the US smartphone market increased 9% YoY in Q2. In addition, smartphone revenue reached $17.5 billion for the quarter, up 11% YoY.Apple and Samsung perform well in Q2
Sales of the iPhone 16e, plus successful device marketing and carrier promotions, supported Apple's sell-through increase of 10% YoY in Q2, according to Counterpoint Research. The firm noted that Verizon's Red Hot Deal Days and T-Mobile's switcher deal, offering a free iPhone 16 Pro without a trade-in, were among the standout promotions.
Verizon said its "Best Value Guarantee" has also been important in boosting handset upgrades and customer satisfaction. The guarantee includes a three-year price lock on all myPlan and MyHome network plans; a free phone with any trade-in from Apple, Google or Samsung; free satellite text messaging; and additional discount and streaming service options.
A recent example of strong carrier promotions was during the launch of Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold7 and Flip 7 handsets, which are new, thinner foldable handsets from the device maker. The Big Three carriers, plus Boost Mobile, Xfinity Mobile and Comcast Business Mobile, all offered $1,100 off the Z Fold 7 with an eligible device trade-in and/or new line.
Indeed, Samsung experienced a solid sell-through increase of 16% YoY, which Counterpoint said was a result of increased demand for its flagship models and device promotions from service providers. The S25 Edge, launched in May, also contributed slightly to the increase in sales.
Related:Samsung flips the script on device upgrades with Galaxy S25 Edge
During the launch of the S25, Samsung ramped up promotion of its Galaxy Club program in addition to offering other promotions such as free streaming services with a direct purchase of the S25 from Samsung. The Galaxy Club is an add-on subscription program for customers to obtain future discounts and device credits.Some smaller players can't take the heat
Market domination by larger OEMs is partly a result of how many smartphones are sold in the US through the service provider channel, Runar Bjørhovde, senior analyst at Canalys, told Light Reading recently. Smaller players don't have the same financial backing to take part in the service provider channel or open their own brick-and-mortar stores to sell directly to consumers, he explained.
Smaller players like Nothing are starting to scale up in retail stores such as Best Buy, but making headway in the US hinges on selling smartphones through the carrier channel, said Bjørhovde. Nothing has also focused on selling directly to consumers via the company's website, in addition to sales through Amazon and Walmart.
Meanwhile, Nothing is performing well in other markets such as India and China – the company's manufacturing base is split between those countries, with slightly more manufacturing in India.
Related:iPhone 16e outperforms iPhone 16 for AT&T, Verizon users – Ookla
Other small smartphone players have been less fortunate. HMD recently exited the US market, and the company's sell-through dropped 41% YoY, according to Counterpoint. HMD, which licenses the Nokia brand for new phones and tablets, initially announced plans to scale back its US operations in July.
Research Director Jeff Fieldhack said that HMD's exit presents an opportunity for other smartphone vendors to fill the gap in the low-end smartphone market, and Motorola and TCL are among the brands well-positioned to step in.
